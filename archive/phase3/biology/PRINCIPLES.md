# Developmental principles

## Relative activity can select which connections survive

In developing mouse retinogeniculate circuits, retinal ganglion cell inputs from both eyes initially overlap. Before eye opening (before about postnatal day 10), spontaneous retinal waves refine them into eye-specific territories. The relative timing matters: activity is synchronized within each retina but asynchronous between retinas. Blocking activity in both eyes or experimentally synchronizing them prevents normal segregation; making the two eyes' activity asynchronous optogenetically can advance it. When only one eye's activity is blocked or increased, its territory shrinks or expands relative to the other eye. These results support activity-dependent competition: some synapses are weakened and removed while correlated competitors are maintained and strengthened.

Source: Faust, Gunner & Schafer, "Mechanisms governing activity-dependent synaptic pruning in the mammalian CNS," *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 22 (2021), 657-673, doi: [10.1038/s41583-021-00507-y](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8541743/). Review opened with `shared/lit.py fetch`; the timing and perturbation claims above are in "Spontaneous neural activity-driven synapse pruning" and its retinogeniculate discussion.

Digital translation to test: let co-active units compete for a postsynaptic unit using only local pre/post timing traces, normalize incoming weights, and remove persistently weak connections. This supplies selection without a world reward or backpropagation. Prediction: inputs with distinguishable temporal relations to postsynaptic activity will specialize; identical/synchronized inputs will remain hard to separate. Ablate timing dependence and pruning separately. The demonstration in `biology/pruning_demo.py` is a toy rule check, not evidence from the organism judge.

## Homeostatic gain control preserves learned distinctions

Turrigiano et al. measured activity-dependent scaling in neocortical cultures: chronic activity blockade increased miniature excitatory postsynaptic current (mEPSC) amplitude, while blocking GABA inhibition initially raised firing; over **48 hours** mEPSC amplitudes decreased and firing returned close to control. The change affected each synapse approximately in proportion to its initial strength. Correlation can therefore change relative weights while a cell-local activity error rescales all incoming weights to keep total drive in range.

Source: Turrigiano et al., "Activity-dependent scaling of quantal amplitude in neocortical neurons," *Nature* 391, 892-896 (1998), PMID 9495341, abstract opened with `shared/lit.py fetch` via NCBI eutils. The mechanism and 48-hour result are also reviewed in Turrigiano, "Homeostatic Synaptic Plasticity: Local and Global Mechanisms for Stabilizing Neuronal Function," *Cold Spring Harb Perspect Biol* 4:a005736 (2012), PMCID PMC3249629, opened with `shared/lit.py fetch`.

Digital translation to test: each unit maintains an exponentially averaged activity target without a reward signal, then multiplies all incoming weights by `target / mean_activity`. This preserves ratios and information in relative weights while preventing runaway growth or collapse. Ablate gain correction; prediction: total activity stays bounded with scaling but drifts without it. `biology/homeostasis_demo.py` is a deterministic toy check, not organism-judge evidence.

## Novelty opens growth; repeated local use stabilizes the new structure

In vivo two-photon imaging of mouse motor cortex found **10.6 +/- 1.1%** new dendritic spines within one hour of the first successful forelimb training session, versus **4.7 +/- 0.6%** in handled controls (Xu et al., *Nature* 462, 915-919, 2009; open full text: PMCID PMC2844762). Spine formation correlated with successful reaches (`r^2 = 0.77`), while unskilled activity did not increase formation. Spines stabilized during adolescent learning persisted into adulthood; after a later novel task, **95.6 +/- 7.7%** of the previously stabilized spines remained.

Digital translation: maintain a local prediction of recent input; prediction error/novelty temporarily raises the probability of adding a connection from currently active inputs. Increase a new connection's stability only through repeated local pre/post co-activity, and decay unused candidates. This gives growth and memory without reward or backpropagation: novelty proposes, recurrence selects. Ablate the novelty gate while keeping the same local stabilization rule. In `biology/novelty_stabilization_demo.py` (20 seeds, two 8-input ensembles), the novel switch formed 1.100 connections with the gate versus 0.250 without; after 60 further steps, 7.950 versus 7.450 novel inputs remained, and final synapses were 15.600 versus 15.050. This is a toy rule check, not an organism-judge result.

## Neighbor suppression turns small biases into sparse fate choices

During inner-ear development, slight stochastic increases in Neurog1 in presumptive neuroblasts promote Dll1 expression. Dll1 activates Notch in adjacent cells, reducing their Neurog1 and neuroblast fate. Inhibiting Notch in chick otocysts with DAPT causes excessive neuroblast delamination, including from the normally non-neurogenic center (Brown & Groves, *Biomolecules* 10, 370, 2020, doi: 10.3390/biom10030370; full text opened with `shared/lit.py fetch`, PMCID PMC7175228). The review describes the mechanism and perturbation qualitatively; it reports no cell-count effect size for this experiment.

Digital translation: let a unit's small local bias toward a role induce a suppressive signal to nearby units competing for that role. This can turn noisy differences into distributed specialization without a global winner or reward. Ablate neighbor suppression and compare both role density and neighboring co-activation. In `biology/lateral_inhibition_demo.py` (100 seeds, 64 units), inhibition produced mean role activity density 0.2648 and neighbor-pair activity 0.0428 versus 0.4988 and 0.2486 without inhibition. The toy supports reduced local redundancy, but also reduces total role activity; adding multiple alternative roles or a local resource constraint is the next test. This is not organism-judge evidence.

## Local survival signals can prune excess cells, with redundant death pathways

In mouse embryos, loss of the pro-apoptotic regulator HRK significantly reduced TUNEL-positive and cleaved-caspase-3-positive cells in dorsal root ganglia (DRG) at embryonic days **12.5** and **15.5**. Yet HRK loss did not significantly lower apoptosis in the DRG of Bcl-x knockout embryos at E12.5; the study describes widespread apoptosis in both Bcl-x knockout genotypes. Thus development uses regulated cell removal, but no single death trigger accounts for all removal. The paper does not report a convenient pooled percentage effect in its abstract/text, so this is a qualitative perturbation result, not a numeric effect-size claim.

Source: Ghosh et al., "Deficiency of Pro-apoptotic Hrk Attenuates Programmed Cell Death in the Developing Murine Nervous System but Does Not Affect Bcl-x Deficiency-Induced Neuron Apoptosis," *J Histochem Cytochem* 59(11), 976-983 (2011), doi: [10.1369/0022155411424311](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3261623/). Full text and results sections opened with `shared/lit.py fetch`, PMCID PMC3261623.

Digital translation to test: give each unit a local maintenance budget or survival trace, supplied by useful local activity and shared among nearby competitors. Low sustained support raises removal probability; vacancies allow local growth, while independent maintenance routes make removal robust to loss of one signal. Ablate support competition and pathway redundancy separately. In a local pruning assay (`biology/redundant_maintenance_demo.py`, 100 seeds, 256 units, 300 steps), two independent Bernoulli support routes (p=0.4 each) left 0.0655 of units alive, versus 0.0117 when the routes were perfectly correlated and 0.0002 when both were off. Each single-route ablation left about 0.01 alive. The assay still nearly extinguishes the population; this supports only the narrow prediction that route independence buffers pruning under this hazard rule. It is hand-designed and is not evidence from the organism judge. The earlier `biology/trophic_competition_demo.py` also remains a toy rule check, not evidence of biological transfer.
