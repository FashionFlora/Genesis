# Biological principles for GENESIS

Working notes for the discovery phase. Each entry separates observed biology from a proposed digital abstraction. This is not a claim that the brain uses one universal mechanism.

## 1. Activity-dependent competitive persistence

**Observed mechanism.** Developing circuits first form excess inputs; a subset is later maintained and strengthened while other inputs are eliminated. In mouse retinogeniculate development, retinal ganglion cell (RGC) inputs compete for territory. During peak pruning, microglia engulf presynaptic inputs through a phagocytic pathway involving microglial CR3 and synaptic C3. Manipulating activity biases which input is removed: TTX-silenced inputs are engulfed more than vehicle controls, while after forskolin increases activity the vehicle inputs are engulfed more than the more-active inputs. The reported comparisons were significant at `P < 0.04` (TTX: `n=4` mice; forskolin: `n=5`). Engulfment is high at mouse postnatal day 5 (P5), substantially lower by P9, and much of this pruning is complete by P9/P10. Blocking CR3/C3 signaling produces sustained connectivity deficits in this system.

**Digital implication.** If the stream benchmark imposes a connection cap, compare candidate edges against *local competitors*: repeatedly weak relative activity can lower an edge's persistence, while a repeatedly stronger competitor survives. Measure false-edge lifetime and recovery after regime switches alongside prediction error and edge count. A probationary edge is a reasonable digital test, but the study does **not** show temporary random sprouts, sampling active partners, or a hard synapse cap. The biological evidence is limited to a specific developmental circuit and pruning window.

**Source opened with `lit.py fetch`.** Schafer et al. (2012), “Microglia Sculpt Postnatal Neural Circuits in an Activity and Complement-Dependent Manner,” *Neuron* 74:691–705, PMCID [PMC3528177](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3528177/).

## 2. A niche signal can switch stem cells between proliferation and quiescence

**Observed mechanism.** Piltti et al. report that extracellular C1q acts through BAI1 in human neural stem cells (hNSCs). C1q at circulating-plasma-associated concentrations of `100–300 nM` reduced the proliferating fraction; `200 nM` promoted G0/G1 arrest without increased cell death. After exposure of up to `48 h`, proliferation rebounded when cells returned to ordinary growth conditions. The proposed mechanism has two branches: BAI1-dependent MDM2–p53 signaling suppresses cell-cycle progression, while internalized C1q–BAI1 lowers mitochondrial p32 and shifts metabolism from oxidative phosphorylation toward aerobic glycolysis. RNA-seq showed increased glycolysis transcripts after `48 h` at `200 nM`, with lower oxygen-consumption and higher extracellular-acidification rates. In a mouse spinal-cord-injury transplantation model, lesion C1q peaked `3–24 h` after injury; transplanted BAI1-knockout hNSCs yielded more human cells at `2 weeks` than wild-type cells (`n=7` mice/group).

**Digital implication.** A slow, reversible niche/condition signal can gate whether a unit keeps dividing or remains quiescent, separately from its fast local learning signal. Test whether this preserves a finite pool during a regime shift and permits re-entry when conditions change. Do not equate the C1q signal with prediction error: the study uses an extracellular ligand and a specific BAI1 signaling pathway. Its hNSC and spinal-injury scope does not establish a universal neural growth rule; prolonged C1q priming also reduced self-renewal, so quiescence is not automatically beneficial.

**Source opened with `lit.py fetch`.** Piltti et al. (2025), “C1q drives neural stem cell quiescence by regulating cell cycle and metabolism through BAI1,” *Nature Communications* 16:11386, PMCID [PMC12738564](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12738564/).

## 3. Homeostasis stabilizes activity; it does not select memories

**Observed mechanism.** Synaptic scaling senses a neuron's longer-term firing relative to a set point and changes glutamate-receptor accumulation. In its global form, excitatory synapses are scaled up or down approximately proportionally, preserving relative synaptic strengths while counteracting runaway changes from correlation-based plasticity. Central-neuron activity can recover toward baseline over many hours. The review describes cumulative scaling detectable after about 4–6 hours of activity blockade; TNFα-associated support is relevant to maintaining scaling during prolonged blockade (>24 hours; common experiments use 48 hours). The review also emphasizes that homeostatic mechanisms differ by synapse class, cell type, and developmental stage.

**Digital implication.** Keep a slow activity/set-point trace separate from a fast prediction-error trace. Use the slow trace to stabilize a unit or local circuit, not to decide which individual edge is informative: global proportional scaling preserves edge ratios and therefore cannot by itself remove false edges. For the life theory, this supports a slow stability variable but does not establish that prediction error is biological energy or that it should trigger neuron division.

**Source opened with `lit.py fetch`.** Turrigiano (2012), “Homeostatic Synaptic Plasticity: Local and Global Mechanisms for Stabilizing Neuronal Function,” *Cold Spring Harbor Perspectives in Biology* 4:a005736, PMCID [PMC3249629](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3249629/).

## 4. Candidate unifying principle: differential persistence under constrained support

**Synthesis, not established biological fact.** The observations above suggest a candidate rule worth testing: local structures compete for finite support, and repeated activity biases where support persists. Chater et al. directly tested multi-synapse competition in CA1 slices by glutamate-uncaging `1`, `3`, `7`, or `15` spines on a dendritic branch. The plasticity outcome over tens of minutes depended on cluster size and distance; with `15` stimulated spines, edge spines potentiated more than the `7` middle spines at `30` and `40 min`. Their model used fast Ca-dependent `C` and slower-diffusing `P` molecules. This supports local, spatially shared resource competition, but the two molecular variables are not evidence for one scalar law.

Synaptic tagging and capture offers a complementary analogue for temporary candidates: a stimulated synapse holds a transient tag and can capture plasticity-related products induced elsewhere. In rat hippocampal CA1 experiments summarized by Bin Ibrahim et al., a second pathway stimulated `1 h` after a strongly stimulated pathway still expressed late LTP despite blocking protein synthesis during the second stimulation; the review gives the tag a roughly `2 h` window. This supports transient local eligibility and capture of shared stabilization factors, but does not show that the shared pool is a hard finite budget.

For a compact digital test, let `E_u` be the finite set of candidate outgoing edges from source `u`, with binary endpoint activity `x_v(t)`. For edge `e=(u,v)`, define current coactivity `c_e(t)=x_u(t)x_v(t)` and local competitive signal `s_e(t)=c_e(t) - mean_{j in E_u}(c_j(t))`. Give each edge an integer support count `k_e >= 0`, with `sum_{e in E_u} k_e <= B`; the edge exists exactly when `k_e > 0`. Let `ξ_e(t)` be independent standard normal noise. For fixed `η>0` and `σ>=0`, one allocation update is

```text
k(t+1) = argmin_{q in N_0^|E_u|, sum(q) <= B} ||q - (k(t) + eta*s(t) + sigma*ξ(t))||_2^2
```

Choose the integer vector `q` nearest in Euclidean distance; if an exact tie occurs, break it uniformly at random. The support count is both connection strength and persistence: a count reaching zero is pruned; spare support can be captured by another candidate; noise samples alternatives but does not guarantee useful exploration. Repeated experience changes the allocation and leaves a persistent trace, so edge learning and memory are the same state change at different durations. Since each present edge consumes at least one integer support unit, `B` bounds outgoing edge count. For the first digital test, `B` is an externally supplied capacity constraint; this rule does not derive the budget from metabolism or homeostatic activity. `η`, `σ`, and `B` remain explicit parameters. These are consequences of the proposed update, not claims that biological synapses literally use integer resource tokens or a projected allocation rule. As written, the rule changes synapses over a fixed candidate neuron set; it does not yet derive neuron birth, sleep replay, or developmental timing. It also ignores dendritic distance and the distinct fast/slow molecular dynamics seen by Chater et al.; both are open tests rather than details to assume away.

**Discriminating test.** On stage-1 streams, compare this one-state allocation rule with the current covariance-edge learner and with a fixed-topology control, using identical seeds and cap. Pre-register prediction: after a correlation switch, the new target should gain support while old false edges lose it, with bounded edge count and no worse return-to-old-regime error. Falsify the unifier for this formulation if no shared `eta/sigma` setting yields both recovery and selective pruning, or if memory of the old regime collapses whenever adaptation is fast. Report all seeds and support/edge trajectories; no result has been run yet.

**Biological boundary.** Schafer et al. support activity-biased elimination of weaker inputs in one developmental circuit; Chater et al. model local molecular competition among stimulated spines; Turrigiano supports slow activity homeostasis that can preserve relative weights; Piltti et al. show niche-signaled quiescence and metabolic change in hNSCs; Bin Ibrahim et al. review transient synaptic tags capturing shared plasticity products. These sources do not demonstrate one finite-support variable that causes growth, pruning, learning, memory, and exploration. C1q appears in both synaptic pruning and hNSC quiescence, but the opened studies describe different pathways and outcomes (C3/CR3-mediated engulfment versus complement-cascade-independent BAI1 signaling). I found no evidence in these sources that neural structures explicitly optimize a code-length or compression-cost objective. The unifier is a testable abstraction motivated by partial overlaps, not a literature claim.

## 5. Canalization: robust function can come from variable wiring

**Observed mechanism and boundary.** A useful distinction is between inherited developmental rules and the circuit that emerges from them. Biswas et al. describe circuit assembly as the coordinated action of conserved genetic and experience-dependent mechanisms: genetic sequence constrains developmental responses, while sensory experience and ongoing cell interactions affect the trajectory. These constraints do not encode every mature synapse. In their discussion, the authors propose that stochastic axonal and dendritic branch growth repeatedly forms candidate synapses that may persist or be eliminated, and that δ2-protocadherins bias this exploratory sampling. The growth/persistence/elimination account is an interpretation supported by prior work, not a mechanism directly isolated by their imaging experiment.

In the larval zebrafish optic tectum, the first five population-activity modes explained `89.9 ± 1.8%` of response variance (`n=9` wild-type fish), and trial-to-trial correlations exceeded `0.9` across all five modes. Loss of either δ-protocadherin Pcdh19 or Pcdh17 made visually evoked dynamics less stereotyped across individuals; the mutant groups were pcdh19 indel `n=12`, pcdh19 promoter deletion `n=9`, and pcdh17 indel `n=13`. Their deviations were stochastic rather than one consistent alternate circuit; pooled within-group pairwise correlations were lower than wild type (`p<0.001`). Two Pcdh19-null alleles also differed in phenotype severity despite both lacking detectable protein. The authors suggest mutation-induced compensation as one possible explanation, not a demonstrated cause. This is direct evidence for canalization of evoked population dynamics and decanalization after these perturbations. Individual connectomes were not measured in this experiment, so wiring variability remains an interpretation rather than its direct readout.

**Candidate unifying abstraction.** Let inherited developmental parameters be `θ`, initial state be `s₀`, and environmental/activity history be `e₁:T`; the developing system follows `sₜ₊₁ = f_θ(sₜ,eₜ,ξₜ)`. Gene expression and circuit state remain dynamic; `θ` represents the inherited part of the transition law, not every molecular concentration as a fixed constant. Its task-relevant outcome is `F(s₁:T,e₁:T)`. Given a reference pair `(θ₀,s₀₀)`, choose a functional distance `d_F`, tolerance `ε`, and a declared perturbation distribution `Π_Δ` over a bounded range `Δ` of `θ` and `s₀`. Call the process `(ε,ρ,Δ)`-canalized if

```text
F₀ = F(θ₀,s₀₀,e,ξ₀),   F' = F(θ',s₀',e,ξ')
P_{(θ',s₀')~Π_Δ, ξ₀,ξ'}[d_F(F',F₀) > ε] ≤ ρ,
```

where `ξ₀` and `ξ'` are independent draws of developmental noise and the sensory history `e` is matched.

This distinguishes robustness of function from identity of the final graph: the candidate predicts that different neuron/synapse realizations may lie in the same functional basin, but the cited experiment did not measure those graph differences. This is a robustness concept and a formal lens for a digital hypothesis, not by itself a learning law or a claim that the brain computes `d_F` or optimizes an explicit robustness objective. The opened studies show no explicit posterior over graph alternatives or learned code for structural waiting times; the stochastic-sampling analogy is about branch/synapse formation and persistence, not Bayesian architecture inference.

**Digital implication and falsifiable test.** A plausible compact abstraction is *stochastic candidate formation plus biased persistence*: random local growth supplies structural exploration; one local persistence state lets repeated interaction stabilize some candidates and remove others; the surviving structure carries history. This connects synaptic growth, pruning, exploratory sampling, and structural memory without a separate random-probe timer. For H004 specifically, no opened source shows that resource amount or packet identity itself chooses an edge variant at cell division. Fission can gate how many variants a separate copying/variation process produces, but H004's uniform daughter-edge draw remains an independent variation law. The closest neural analogue is activity-dependent candidate growth plus biased persistence, not division-linked mutation; Chater et al. (2024; §4) show local shared molecular competition changing spine plasticity, not generating partner identity. These papers do not demonstrate that one mechanism controls stem-cell division, metabolic homeostasis, and all forms of memory. To test robustness, treat inherited constants as the local transition law and its operating ranges; let structure and learned state emerge from activity, interaction, and history. On an identical stage-1 stream, run many initial seeds and controlled perturbations to developmental parameters, initialization, and candidate availability. Measure predictive-trajectory variance and recovery alongside graph/unit divergence. Canalization predicts that some bounded perturbations can produce divergent structures with relatively stable function, while disabling or removing a genuine buffering interaction should increase functional variance. Falsify it for a tested range if small perturbations produce no stable functional basin. Report perturbation ranges and failures. This gives life a robustness test for `B=I-C-L` and axioms a criterion beyond edge survival: does the same rule preserve function across perturbed starts, and does a perturbed prior alter topology more than function?

## 6. Endogenous wanting: viability error needs action-conditioned prediction

**Observed evidence and limits.** Allostasis is predictive regulation of internal body states, not merely restoration after a measured deviation. The human fMRI study by Kleckner et al. constructed an allostatic-interoceptive network from macaque tracing and human resting-state samples (`N=280` and `N=270`); in a separate sample (`N=41`), stronger connectivity between system hubs covaried with better performance on an implicit autonomic interoception index. These are network/association results, not a demonstration that a neural scalar computes utility or causes a particular action. The review of early sensory-motor development by Chen & Monroy describes motor milestones as changing what infants can sample: in one cited cohort of `50` infants aged 11 and 13 months, walkers more often accessed distant objects, carried them, and approached caregivers to share. It also reports a locomotion-training study (`n=30` six-month-olds) that improved mirror-object discrimination relative to controls. Gliga's review finds that infant movement amount and variability can rise when novel information is available, but emphasizes that direct evidence that this variability itself improves infant task learning is still scarce. Thus exploratory variation is a plausible source of informative interventions, not proof of a built-in curiosity objective.

Developmental play is similarly informative but domain-specific. Marquardt et al. prevented rat play while preserving other social contact from postnatal day 26 to 40, or day 21 to 45 in the longer condition; across all experiments they used `196` rats. Later effects were mainly socio-sexual and male-biased, while anxiety-like behavior, object memory, sex preference, and social recognition were not changed. Play therefore develops particular action and social capacities; this study does not show that all juvenile exploration is driven by one intrinsic reward.

**Candidate synthesis, not a single established law.** Let `v` denote variables required for continued self-maintenance and `V` their viable region. Distance from `V` gives an endogenous direction of need, but not an action: identical passive inputs can coexist with opposite action consequences. An issued action copy paired with predicted and observed sensory/interoceptive change supplies causal evidence about which intervention moves `v` toward `V`. Developmental movement variability and play can provide diverse interventions; neither is itself evidence of a universal curiosity reward. In the mouse retinogeniculate system, spontaneous retinal activity and experimentally altered relative activity are linked to eye-specific refinement, and microglia preferentially engulfed weaker inputs after activity manipulation (TTX versus forskolin comparisons, `n=4` and `n=5`, `P<0.04`; pruning high at P5, much lower by P9). This gives a biological route from activity to selective persistence, but not a demonstrated route from organismal need to synaptic selection. The proposed bridge is: need sets the direction of useful change; action-copy/feedback estimates its causal effect; local competition preserves predictive action-outcome paths that repeatedly reduce need and weakens alternatives. Autopoiesis is the boundary condition that makes some internal variables matter (continued maintenance of the organization that generates them), not a specified neural algorithm. Allen & Friston discuss autopoiesis and predictive processing theoretically; they do not show that one free-energy law empirically generates motivation, circuit growth, and pruning.

**Digital implication.** Represent need as distance from a viable region, maintain action-conditioned local predictions from issued-action copies and returned internal/external signals, and compare predicted need reduction across actions. Use naturally variable early actions to identify effects; update each action edge from its own prediction error and allocate finite local support by predicted viability contribution. This couples spontaneous intervention, causal prediction, endogenous selection, and structural persistence without a separately supplied reward. Prediction: paired environments with the same passive stream but reversed action effects defeat passive selection; a noisy intervention phase should learn opposite action-outcome edges and preserve only the locally useful edge. This is a proposed abstraction; it does not establish that infant motor noise is goal-directed or that biological pruning optimizes a viability score.

**Toy results (actually run).** The minimal identifiability case in `biology/viability_probe.py` has identical passive signatures in `2/2` opposite-effect worlds: fixed passive choice is correct in `1/2`, intervention feedback in `2/2`. A noisy extension in `biology/viability_pruning_probe.py` alternates two early actions for 20 trials in each of 80 worlds (`40` paired environments; observation SD `0.025`), estimates action-conditioned internal change online, and retains an edge only when predicted to reduce deviation from the viable point. It chose the truly helpful action in `80/80` worlds and retained exactly that edge in `80/80`; passive-only baseline accuracy is `0.50`. Result: `biology/viability_pruning_probe_result.json`. This is a controlled identifiability demonstration, not evidence for biological adequacy: the exploratory schedule and viability target are supplied, dynamics are stationary, and structural support is assigned directly from estimated benefit. It does not show self-generated wanting, developmental exploration, or a judge result.

**Control result (actually run).** `biology/action_pruning_controls.py` tests 100 matched pairs of opposite action-effect worlds (200 total; 40 trials/world; observation SD `0.04`). Uniformly sampled interventions plus local competitive support reached `200/200` correct choices and `200/200` exact single-edge retention. A fixed passive action reached `100/200` correct choices. With interventions but no pruning, choice accuracy remained `200/200`, while exact single-edge retention fell to `0/200` (both edges remain). Thus in this toy, interventions identify causal action effects; pruning is specifically needed for sparse structural selection, not for choosing well once both action models have been learned. Result: `biology/action_pruning_controls_result.json`. This still supplies the viability target and assumes stationary action effects; it does not model organismal self-maintenance or developmental circuits.

**Setpoint reversal result (actually run).** `biology/need_reversal_probe.py` learns the same two opposite action effects from 12 alternating interventions per world, then tests paired blocks with the internal target at opposite values. With Gaussian observation noise SD `0.04`, the action selected by predicted distance-to-target and its single retained action edge reversed with need in `200/200` blocks; a fixed-left action baseline was correct in `100/200`. Result: `biology/need_reversal_probe_result.json`. This isolates a useful property of the proposed rule: a need signal can reverse which learned action path persists without changing the action model or introducing an external reward. It is still a toy: setpoint, actions, and exploratory sampling are supplied; dynamics are stationary and the edge is chosen directly from predicted benefit. It does not explain where the first variability comes from or demonstrate autopoiesis.

**Noise/sample-size boundary (actually run).** `biology/need_probe_noise_sweep.py` tests 25 combinations of observation noise (`0.025`–`0.8`) and intervention counts (`4`–`40` per world), each on `100` worlds (50 opposite-effect pairs). At SD `0.4`, four trials gave `0.71` action accuracy and `0.35` correct single-edge retention; 40 trials gave `1.00` and `0.98`. At SD `0.8`, even 40 trials gave `0.87` choice accuracy and `0.59` correct retention. Result: `biology/need_probe_noise_sweep_result.json`. This confirms a toy-level boundary: structural pruning needs enough accumulated causal evidence and is less reliable than choosing between actions. The alternating intervention schedule and viability target remain supplied, so the run does not show endogenous motivation or self-generated exploration.

**Unscheduled variability and drifting target (actually run).** `biology/endogenous_wanting_probe.py` compares an uncertainty-driven stochastic policy, a greedy action-conditioned predictor, and a fixed action in 200 worlds. Two actions have opposite effects on an internal state; the target reverses at step 100, feedback noise SD is `0.08`, and local edge support decays while the currently beneficial action can reinforce its own edge. After the reversal, both learned policies chose the newly helpful action on `100%` of the last 40 steps; the fixed policy scored `49.5%`. Mean first beneficial response was `0.02` steps for uncertainty-driven and `0.025` for greedy, versus `50.51` for fixed. A single correct edge remained in `100%` of learned worlds versus `49.5%` for fixed. Result: `biology/endogenous_wanting_probe_result.json`. This falsifies the toy claim that uncertainty-driven variability is needed in this easy, fixed-action-effects model: greedy prediction performs as well or slightly better. It does not test a real metabolic budget (all policies receive 200 action opportunities), self-maintenance, or developmental learning; target, candidate actions, and candidate edges are supplied. Digital implication: start with a viability error plus an action-copy-indexed effect estimate; allow exploratory action variation only when the effect model is uncertain or stale, then let repeated local evidence govern edge persistence. Compare against greedy prediction under a genuine action/energy budget and abrupt effect changes before attributing value to endogenous exploration.

## 7. Wanting as viability-directed causal inference

**Biological synthesis, not a single established mechanism.** “Wanting” need not be an extra reward signal: a sensed departure from viable conditions supplies a direction of correction, while the organism's action repertoire and learned action consequences supply candidate means. Homeostatic control corrects current deviations; allostasis anticipates changing demands and therefore depends on interoceptive prediction. In circuit development, activity biases persistence and elimination, but the specific pruning evidence is from the mouse retinogeniculate system during a restricted developmental window (Schafer et al., §1). Juvenile sensorimotor behavior supplies varied action–feedback pairings; the reviewed developmental evidence links movement and sensory experience to later cognition, while play-deprivation effects are domain- and sex-specific, not proof that play is a universal reward-free exploration engine. An efference copy / corollary discharge is a useful control concept: predict sensory consequences of a motor command, then compare prediction with reafferent feedback. The opened hopping paper is a conceptual biomechanical model, not decisive evidence for a general neural law. Autopoiesis supplies the boundary condition: a living system must maintain the organization that makes its continued operation possible; it is not a demonstrated synaptic learning algorithm.

**Candidate digital principle.** Couple one viability deviation to one action-conditioned prediction loop. Let `x` be sensed internal state, `x*` a viable region, `a` a sampled action, and `m_a` the local estimate of its sensory consequence. The deviation `x* - x` biases which predicted consequence is useful; compare `m_a` with the actual post-action change to update only the action-tagged path (the digital analogue of efference-copy credit assignment). Candidate paths can be generated by the system's own unresolved prediction errors or intrinsic activity; repeated prediction-consistent reduction of viability deviation raises local support, while inactivity or repeated harmful prediction errors lets support decay. Shared support competition supplies a finite structural budget, linking learning, persistence and pruning. A slow viability trace may change with need (allostasis); it must not be conflated with the fast action-effect estimate. This offers a concrete answer to “why act without external reward”: action has intrinsic direction because consequences alter the conditions of continued self-maintenance. It does not prove that organisms compute scalar viability error or optimize it explicitly.

**Toy boundary result (actually run).** `biology/viability_intervention_boundary.py` tests 200 paired-sign worlds with two opposite action effects of magnitude `0.2`, need magnitude `0.2`, and observation SD `0.08`. With one initial action probe, action choice was correct in `200/200` worlds but exact single-edge retention only `100/200`; two probes gave `200/200` choice and `199/200` correct single-edge retention; four or more gave `200/200` for both. The task structure makes one action sufficient to infer which member of an assumed opposite-effect pair is useful, while both action consequences must be sampled to establish exact sparse structure. Result: `biology/viability_intervention_boundary_result.json`. This tests the separation between fast control and slower structural identification, not organismal metabolism, spontaneous play, or neuron birth. The prescribed target, binary action set, and paired-effect prior make the outcome an identifiability boundary, not evidence that living systems reach these rates.

**Falsifiable digital test.** On a stream with an internally sensed resource that depletes under activity and replenishes under useful behavior, compare (a) action-conditioned consequence prediction plus local support decay, (b) the same controller without action tags, and (c) no structural pruning. Give each the same finite action/resource budget; introduce unannounced action-effect drift. Record resource survival, recovery time, exploratory action count, false-edge lifetime, and predictive score. The principle earns support only if endogenous need-driven interventions recover while maintaining resource, and local support removes false edges without losing recovery capacity. Existing probes use a supplied target and action chances; `biology/endogenous_wanting_probe.py` found greedy prediction as good as uncertainty sampling in its easy stationary-effect toy, so stochastic exploration is not yet justified as a required component.

**Quantitative grounding from opened sources.** Kleckner et al. associate a human allostatic/interoceptive network with peripheral regulation using macaque tract tracing and human fMRI samples `N=280` and `N=270`; a further `N=41` sample links stronger hub connectivity with an implicit autonomic interoception measure. This supports a body-state control architecture, but does not demonstrate a single scalar need signal or causality. In the mouse retinogeniculate system, Schafer et al. found microglial engulfment peaked at P5 and pruning was largely complete by P9/P10; engulfment varied with retinal activity (TTX-silenced inputs were more engulfed than controls, while forskolin-enhanced inputs were spared relative to less-active controls; `n=4` and `n=5` mice, respectively, `P<0.04`). This is evidence for activity-biased developmental removal in one circuit, not a generic edge-update law. Turrigiano's review reports that activity blockade can produce cumulative synaptic scaling detectable after roughly `4–6 h`; scaling is approximately proportional and preserves relative synaptic strengths, so it stabilizes activity but cannot alone select which edge to prune. The early-development review by Chen & Monroy summarizes a study of `50` infants at 11 and 13 months where walking infants accessed distant objects, carried objects, and approached caregivers to share more often than crawlers; in a locomotion-training study, `30` pre-locomotor 6-month-olds were randomized to training/control and training improved mirror-image object discrimination. Another reported cohort needed at least `12 weeks` of crawling versus `7 weeks` of walking experience to consistently solve landmark search. These results support action-generated data and experience-dependent calibration, not an innate scalar reward. The opened efference-copy article (Kim et al.) is a conceptual hopping-control model; treat action-tagged prediction as a useful engineering analogy, not established universal neural circuitry. Autopoiesis (Allen & Friston) motivates a system boundary defined by self-maintenance, but is theoretical and supplies neither a quantitative biological law nor a synaptic algorithm.

**Finite-resource drift probe (actually run).** `biology/viability_budget_drift.py` gives 200 seeded worlds two opposite-effect actions, a sensed need, an energy meter, noisy feedback (`SD=0.02`), and an unannounced effect reversal at step 120. A tagged action-effect predictor was compared with an action-pooled predictor and fixed action; every attempted action costs energy, while need-reducing action outcomes replenish it. The tagged mode averaged `238.2/240` actions, final energy `0.195`, absolute final need `0.109`, and `63.0%` correct choices after reversal (mean five-correct recovery `42.6` steps). Pooled and fixed modes each averaged `164.9` actions, final energy `0.0033`, final absolute need `1.0`, and `10.9%` post-reversal accuracy. The result file is `biology/viability_budget_drift_result.json`. This supports action-conditioned credit in this toy, not endogenous motivation in animals: scalar need, energy, two actions, and reversal are supplied, and the hand-designed replenishment rule makes helpful behavior self-sustaining. It does not compare stochastic exploration or validate pruning; combine with the earlier action/pruning control and retest across parameter sweeps before proposing a candidate mechanism.

## 8. Efference-copy credit has a temporal window

**Biological grounding and limits.** Kim et al. describe a one-degree-of-freedom forward model for hopping: a copy of the motor command estimates the movement-generated ground-reaction signal, so the controller can cancel expected reafference and remain sensitive to external disturbance. Their paper frames this as a proposed control model and uses dynamic programming; it is not a neural recording or causal manipulation establishing a general mechanism. Its useful constraint is temporal: a command copy has to remain associated with its predicted consequence until sensory feedback arrives, otherwise self-generated input and external disturbance are confounded.

**Digital implication.** Keep a short eligibility queue of issued action IDs and predicted consequences; when delayed feedback arrives, update only its matching action-effect estimate and local support. A last-action label or untagged pooled error will misassign credit when feedback overlaps subsequent actions. The trace should expire after a bounded window to avoid stale credit. This gives a direct connection between action prediction and structural selection: delayed but correctly tagged prediction errors can reinforce/prune the corresponding local edge, while instantaneous activity alone cannot identify causation.

**Delayed-credit robustness probe (actually run).** `biology/efference_robustness_probe.py` compares feedback assigned to its issued action with a last-action control across feedback delays `0, 1, 4, 8`, noise SD `0, 0.2, 0.5`, and action-repeat probabilities `0, 0.5, 0.9`; each of 36 conditions has 40 seeded worlds of 240 steps, with action effects reversed at step 120. Mean post-switch choice accuracy across conditions was `0.894` for tagged credit and `0.694` for last-action credit. At delay `0` they tied (`0.897`); at delays `1/4/8`, tagged accuracy was `0.896/0.894/0.890` versus `0.477/0.729/0.673`. Result: `biology/efference_robustness_probe_result.json`. This shows tolerance to the tested delay/noise/action-persistence ranges in a supplied two-action reversal task. The outcome and reversal are externally specified, the estimator rate is fixed, and no edge birth, support competition, or pruning was measured; this is not evidence for a biological universal or the whole digital principle.

**Mechanistic implication and boundary.** A copy is useful because it labels which command could have caused a later sensory change; its prediction supplies an expected self-generated component, while the residual between prediction and feedback marks what was unpredicted. For a digital circuit, preserve `(edge/action, predicted consequence, issue time)` only through the feedback horizon, update that matching predictor from its residual, and let structural support depend on repeated predictive usefulness relative to local alternatives. Expiration bounds stale credit, but a queue of exact identities is an engineering implementation, not a demonstrated neural data structure. The opened Kim et al. article explicitly proposes this comparison in a hopping control model; it does not report neural recordings, a pruning mechanism, or a general experimental law. The numerical sweep above therefore tests causal attribution under delay, not the proposed support-to-pruning link.

**Calibration boundary (synthetic probe, actually run).** `biology/efference_calibration_probe.py` estimated an action-conditioned sensory gain from 60 calibration trials, then detected an external `+0.5` pulse among movement-related signals (`±0.5`, noise SD `0.15`) in 200 seeded worlds. At nominal gain, raw magnitude thresholding had precision/recall/FPR `0.067/0.514/0.845`; residual thresholding gave `0.837/0.852/0.019`. With the applied model gain at `0.8×` and `1.2×`, residual FPR was `0.047` and `0.046`; at `0.5×` and `1.5×`, it rose to `0.249` and `0.250`. Thus subtracting predicted reafference helps only while its forward model is reasonably calibrated; systematic gain error leaves a false residual that can look like external novelty and could misdirect any downstream plasticity. Result: `biology/efference_calibration_probe_result.json`. This thresholded toy supplies action contexts, pulse magnitude, and a single scalar gain; it tests no animal circuit or synaptic support. **Digital implication:** track calibration error separately from novelty/support, and gate structural updates by confidence in the action-conditioned prediction instead of interpreting every large residual as an external cause.

**Source opened with `lit.py fetch`.** Kim et al. (2013), “Efferent Copy and Corollary Discharge Motor Control Behavior Associated with a Hopping Activity,” *Journal of Novel Physiotherapies* 3:167, PMCID [PMC8849611](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8849611/).

**Circuit-level constraint from an experiment.** Schneider, Nelson & Mooney (2014) recorded intracellularly in behaving mice and used optogenetic gain- and loss-of-function to test the pathway: secondary motor cortex (M2) neurons projecting to auditory cortex are active during movement; movement suppresses auditory-cortex excitatory neurons before and during movement, with increased local parvalbumin-positive interneuron activity contributing to that suppression. M2 terminals were sufficient to evoke movement-like auditory-cortex dynamics at rest, and M2 activity was necessary to sustain movement-related dynamics. This supports a predictive motor-context gate implemented through a specific inter-area projection and local inhibition; it is not evidence that one universal scalar prediction error directly drives synapse pruning. Source opened with `lit.py fetch`: “A synaptic and circuit basis for corollary discharge in the auditory cortex,” *Nature* (2014), doi: [10.1038/nature13724](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13724).

**Quantitative timing and sign constraint (new source opened).** Nelson et al. (2013) mapped the mouse M2-to-auditory-cortex projection and recorded the circuit in open-access experiments (PMCID [PMC3761045](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3761045/)). In slices, M2 stimulation produced both excitation and inhibition in auditory pyramidal cells (`n=15`); the inhibitory current was about `4x` the excitatory current and lagged it by `3.84 +/- 2.70 ms` (`n=8`, `P=0.0051`). In vivo, M2-terminal stimulation was tested in `28` cells from `5` mice: `27/28` cells responded, hyperpolarizing area was `-0.422 +/- 0.408 mV*s` versus depolarizing `0.083 +/- 0.098 mV*s` (about `5x` larger), and spontaneous firing fell from `1.71 +/- 0.99` to `0` spikes/s for about `200 ms` (`n=11`, `P=0.016`). For tone responses (`80 ms`, `80 dB SPL`), the depolarization lasted `100 +/- 107 ms` without M2 input versus `60 +/- 118 ms` with it (`n=27`, `P=0.0002`); the voltage area changed from `267 +/- 361` to `-23 +/- 468 mV/s` (`P=6e-9`). Varying onset timing showed maximal suppression when M2 stimulation preceded sound by `0--100 ms` (`n=7`, `2` mice). This is a concrete local inhibitory gate with a finite lead window, not merely an abstract subtraction.

**Digital consequence and drift probe (actually run).** The forward model should have two outputs: a residual for learning, and a confidence gate for structural changes. `biology/efference_context_drift_probe.py` used `200` seeded worlds, two overlapping action-effect channels, `0.12` noise, `0.5` external pulses on `10%` of post-calibration trials, and an unannounced motor-gain change at step `120` (`0.6x` or `1.4x`). Raw magnitude detection had recall `1.00` but false-positive rate `1.00`; action-tagged residuals reduced FPR to `0.101`/`0.073` (low/high gain) with recall `0.799`/`0.891`. Gating structural novelty while the residual model was uncertain reduced FPR further to `0.031`/`0.029`, at the cost of recall `0.442`/`0.484`. Thus a large residual during motor-model drift should not immediately prune or grow an edge; hold structural support changes until calibration recovers, while retaining the residual for predictor adaptation. Result: `biology/efference_context_drift_probe_result.json`. This is a synthetic two-channel boundary test, not evidence that mice implement this exact confidence variable.

**Two concurrent corollary-discharge computations (new source opened).** In recordings during self-initiated multi-phrase twitter vocalizations from five marmosets, Tsunada et al. analyzed `3,285` auditory-cortex units and separated phrase-locked phasic suppression from sustained tonic suppression. Phasic suppression was broadly expressed regardless of a unit's frequency tuning (consistent with temporal gating); tonic suppression was selective for vocal frequencies and feedback (consistent with a sensory prediction). Some individual neurons showed both components. Frequency-shifted feedback changed activity associated with tonic suppression, while the phasic component varied with phrase timing, arguing against treating all movement-related suppression as one scalar prediction error. Source opened with `lit.py fetch`: Tsunada et al. (2024), “Multiple processes of vocal sensory-motor interaction in primate auditory cortex,” PMCID [PMC11006904](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11006904/).

**Digital implication / boundary.** Represent an action copy with at least two separable effects: a short-lived broad gate around the predicted sensory event, and a content-specific forward model whose mismatch remains available for correction. Let only the latter's action-specific, repeatedly calibrated residual inform edge support; a gate that suppresses expected input is not itself evidence that its generating edge deserves persistence. This is a computational analogy from a primate auditory-cortex study, not evidence of synaptic pruning or a general-purpose two-channel circuit.

**Support-link probe (actually run; new).** `biology/efference_support_window_probe.py` tests whether the action tag remains useful when the same local support rule has a fixed four-step eligibility horizon. In `200` seeded binary-outcome worlds, two candidate edges compete for total support `1.0`; action effects reverse at step `120`, exploration is `0.15`, and the result reports post-switch choice accuracy separately from correct-edge retention and mean edges above a `0.05` support floor. At feedback delays `1/4`, tagged assignment gave choice accuracy `0.749/0.744`, correct-edge retention `0.798/0.793`, and mean edge counts `1.38/1.39`; a last-action control gave `0.489/0.635`, `0.494/0.666`, and `1.76/1.64`. At delay `8`, the four-step tag expired, so the tagged learner did not adapt (`0.073` accuracy, `0` correct-edge retention, `2.00` edges); the last-action control reached `0.587`, `0.610`, and `1.76`. The support totals are bounded but the edge count is not capped at one because both can remain above the floor. Result: `biology/efference_support_window_probe_result.json`. This supports a limit for the *tested digital credit rule*: stale action tags must expire, but shortening the window can stop adaptation when consequences arrive later. Outcomes, reversal, exploration, and support updates are supplied by the toy; it tests no neural growth or pruning.

**Connection to circuit evidence.** Nelson et al.'s mouse M2-to-auditory-cortex experiment (§8 source below) demonstrates a fast, movement-linked inhibitory gate, including strongest suppression for M2 lead times of `0--100 ms`. It measures auditory responses and local inhibition, not lasting changes in synaptic support. A digital implementation should therefore keep transient sensory gating/eligibility distinct from the slower edge-persistence state, and should require repeated tagged evidence before changing structural support. That separation is a proposal prompted by the circuit's timing, not a biological claim that the measured inhibitory gate prunes connections.

**Small digital probe (actually run).** `biology/efference_gate_probe.py` supplied two action contexts with expected sensory components `-0.5/+0.5`, Gaussian noise `SD=0.15`, and one external pulse of `0.5` in each of `200` worlds of `120` trials. The first 60 trials calibrated an action-conditioned predictor; on the remaining trials, thresholding raw sensory magnitude produced recall `0.42`, precision `0.0042`, and false-positive rate `0.842`; thresholding the residual after subtracting predicted reafference produced recall `0.84`, precision `0.226`, and false-positive rate `0.024` (`biology/efference_gate_probe_result.json`). This confirms only the arithmetic consequence in a deliberately separable toy. It does not test biological circuitry, synaptic changes, or robustness to overlapping/mislearned action effects. **Digital implication:** gate the expected sensory component using the issued action context, and expose the residual to novelty or structural-support updates; keep the gating signal and synaptic support as distinct state variables until experiments show how they couple.

**Overlapping-action boundary (synthetic probe).** Nelson et al. (2013; PMCID PMC3761045, opened above) show a movement-timed auditory gate, not that one delayed observation identifies which of several overlapping movements caused it. `biology/efference_overlap_probe.py` mixed consequences of the latest `4` actions, switched effects halfway through `300` steps, and ran `300` seeds. Correct post-switch action accuracy was `0.461` with oldest-tag credit, `0.507` with latest-action credit, and `0.131` when the residual was split by recent action frequency; correct-edge retention was `0.762`, `0.759`, and `0.159`, respectively (`biology/efference_overlap_probe_result.json`). The stipulated synthetic update refutes this particular distributed-credit shortcut, not biological eligibility or predictive control. Digital implication: a temporal trace alone is not causal assignment. With overlapping effects, compare aggregate predictions for alternative action histories and update support only when those histories are distinguishable; test with independently varied action sequences and an explicit delayed-response kernel.

**New cellular constraint: a silent, short-lived eligibility state.** Shindou et al. recorded corticostriatal plasticity in adult mouse striatum and found that pre-before-post activity made D1-neuron synapses eligible for dopamine-triggered potentiation selectively when dopamine arrived `2 s` later; dopamine outside that timing did not potentiate the tested synapses. The trace was associated with transient Ca-permeable AMPA-receptor rectification lasting `2–4 s`. This is direct cellular evidence for a temporary synaptic state that can later be converted into persistent change, but it is dopamine-dependent reinforcement plasticity in an adult preparation, not evidence that endogenous need substitutes for dopamine or that the same time constant generalizes across circuits. Digital implication: separate an ephemeral action/context eligibility variable from long-term edge support; let a later viability-relevant prediction residual gate consolidation as a hypothesis, while learning the eligibility horizon from data instead of copying `2 s`.

**Eligibility-kernel probe (synthetic, actually run).** `biology/eligibility_kernel_probe.py` compared newest-only, four exponential kernels, and a matched four-step box kernel in `300` worlds with independent randomized actions, a four-action delayed outcome mixture, noise SD `0.15`, and an action-effect reversal at step `120`. During the post-switch scoring window, the currently useful action edge ranked correctly in `92.4%` of steps with newest-only credit, versus `63.3%` with the matched box kernel; exponential time constants `0.5/1/2/4` scored `89.8/82.1/71.0/66.2%` (`biology/eligibility_kernel_probe_result.json`). Under this abrupt drift, concentrated recent eligibility adapted better than matching the full outcome kernel; the probe does not show that newest-only attribution is generally correct, and its outcome kernel/reversal are stipulated. This measures ranking from learned action effects, not biological edge retention or pruning. Digital implication: test the credit horizon jointly against consequence latency and environmental drift; do not assume that the best predictor kernel is also the best structural persistence kernel.

**Source opened with `lit.py fetch`.** Shindou et al. **Timing probe (synthetic, actually run).** `biology/efference_timing_probe.py` compares raw sensory thresholding with subtraction of an action-conditioned expected signal across cue-to-sensory offsets. For configured negative offsets (action leads: `-150`, `-50`), detection recall was `0.837` with false-positive rate `0.019`; for coincident or later cues, recall/FPR remained `0.56/0.841`, identical to raw thresholding (`biology/efference_timing_probe_result.json`). This isolates causal ordering in a deliberately separable toy; the model does not test window duration and cannot reproduce the biological `0–100 ms` tuning. Digital implication: an action copy should become eligible only after issuance and expire with the modeled consequence window; future probes should learn that window and test overlapping actions before coupling residuals to edge persistence.

**Direct human circuit and distinct signal roles.** Khalilian-Gourtani et al. recorded ECoG from `8` neurosurgical participants during five speech-production tasks. A directed motor-to-auditory connectivity pattern peaked on average `107.5 ms` before articulation and was replicated across tasks. A passive-listening control in `3` participants replicated comprehension-related patterns but did not show the prearticulatory pattern. Across `73` auditory electrodes, stronger inflow from this prearticulatory pattern correlated with greater speech-induced auditory suppression (`r=0.430`, `P=1.45e-4`). Their timing analysis found the correlation strongest before articulation and flatter during speech, consistent with a predictive motor signal followed by local maintenance of suppression. This is human circuit evidence for a temporally leading, directed signal linked to suppression; the authors note unrecorded subcortical sources cannot be excluded, and correlation does not establish synaptic pruning. Source opened with `lit.py fetch`: Khalilian-Gourtani et al. (2024), “A corollary discharge circuit in human speech,” *PNAS* 121:e2404121121, PMCID [PMC11648673](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11648673/), doi: [10.1073/pnas.2404121121](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2404121121).

**Broad gate versus feature prediction (synthetic probe, actually run).** `biology/efference_gate_prediction_probe.py` compared raw thresholding, a broad gain `0.25` on all sensory channels, subtraction of the predicted action-specific feature, and their serial combination. In `300` worlds per condition, a feature predictor calibrated on `40` of `160` trials reduced false positives from `0.980` to `0.007`, with recall `0.896` for an event overlapping the predicted feature and `0.892` for an orthogonal event. The broad gate alone reduced FPR to `0`, but recall to `0.009` and `0`; the serial combination suppressed both event classes (recall `0`). This constructed example shows why blanket gating and content-specific prediction should be represented separately if preserving unexpected sensory evidence matters. Its two-feature signals, event, gain and threshold are stipulated; it does not model neural inhibition, active feedback correction, or structural change. Digital implication: keep broad sensory gain and action-conditioned expected content as distinct state variables; test whether a residual-based structural update remains selective under prediction error before linking either signal to pruning. Result: `biology/efference_gate_prediction_probe_result.json`.

**Long-horizon representation in cerebellar output.** During pseudo-random manual tracking, Popa, Streng & Ebner analyzed simple-spike activity from `183` monkey Purkinje cells. Position, velocity, and target-position error were encoded both before and after the corresponding behavior; population decoding remained informative in the farthest tested lead and lag bins, `1.5–2.0 s`. The study separates movement kinematics from task error, but records correlations and decoding, not an efference-copy pathway or causal synaptic change. It supports maintaining distinct predicted movement and task-outcome traces across a horizon longer than immediate sensory attenuation; it does not establish that every action needs a two-second trace. **Variable-horizon attribution probe (synthetic, actually run).** `biology/efference_horizon_probe.py` randomized two actions with supplied effects `+1/-1` and feedback delay sampled uniformly from `1–8` steps, with noise SD `0.25` (`300` worlds, `400` steps each). The correct action was selected on `100.0%` of post-warmup feedbacks when each outcome updated its issued-action copy, versus `71.2%` with a fixed four-step label and `50.5%` when credit went to the action currently being issued. Digital implication: retain a time-indexed copy/eligibility trace until feedback rather than hard-coding one latency, and separately test whether that trace should decay or consolidate into edge support.

**Eligibility traces bridge synaptic and behavioural time scales.** Gerstner et al.'s open review distinguishes millisecond spikes from behaviours lasting seconds: pre/post co-activation sets a transient synaptic flag, and a later third factor converts that flag into a weight change. In the reviewed CA1 experiment, ten presynaptic pulses over `1 s` were potentiated when a calcium plateau occurred `1–2 s` before or after; potentiation was about `200%` after `5` pairings. A reviewed striatal experiment converted the flag when dopamine arrived with a delay shorter than `1 min`; a computational hippocampal model used a `2 s` eligibility-trace time constant. These are heterogeneous experiments and a review, not evidence for one universal trace or for efference copy specifically.

**Variable-delay trace/pruning probe (actually run).** `biology/efference_variable_delay_trace_probe.py` ran `300` worlds for `360` steps with action effects reversed at `180`, feedback delays uniformly `1–8`, noise SD `0.10`, and `20%` random interventions. Exact action tags reached post-switch choice `0.841`, correct-edge retention `0.922`, and `1.157` mean edges above the `0.08` floor. Longer traces improved control in this drift task but retained more competing edges (`1.262` at `tau=8` versus `1.157` for exact tags). Digital implication: let the feedback-delay distribution set the trace horizon, and decay structural support separately from the predictive trace; a trace that is too short misses delayed causes, while one that is too broad smears support across co-active edges. This is a supplied binary toy with hand-set support updates, not a biological pruning measurement.

## 9. Developmental movement is an intervention budget, not an external reward

**New biological constraint.** The early-development review by Chen & Monroy reports that motor milestones change the information an infant can create: in a cited cohort of `50` infants at 11 and 13 months, walkers more often reached distant objects, carried objects, and approached caregivers to share than crawlers. A locomotion-training study randomized `30` pre-locomotor six-month-olds; training improved mirror-image object discrimination relative to controls. Another cited cohort required at least `12` weeks of crawling versus `7` weeks of walking experience to solve a landmark-search task consistently. These findings support a causal route from self-generated movement to new sensory data and calibration. They do not show a universal curiosity reward, and the review is a synthesis rather than one common experiment.

**Digital implication.** Treat juvenile variability/play as a temporary *intervention budget*: actions that are not yet chosen for predicted viability can still be issued, but their only persistent consequence is an action-tagged prediction and local support update. Once an edge has enough causal evidence, the same support law can reduce sampling. This makes exploration a phase of identifiability under a viability boundary, rather than a separate reward module. A useful implementation is: retain `(action, predicted interoceptive change, issue time)` until feedback; update that edge with its residual; compare support locally; let the sampling rate fall as support differences become decisive. The biological sources do not establish this exact algorithm.

**Actually run probe.** `biology/developmental_action_sampling_probe.py` used four signed interventions, feedback delay `4`, noise SD `0.08`, an effect reversal at step `80`, and `200` seeds per mode. A varied mode forced all four actions during the first `24` steps and then periodically revisited them; a greedy mode selected from learned estimates. With one local residual/support trace, varied sampling identified the post-reversal best edge in `97.5%` of worlds and retained it in `93.5%`; greedy sampling reached `49.5%` and `50.0%`, respectively. Mean retained-edge count was `2.745` versus `1.0`; varied mode visited all four actions in its initial window, greedy only `1`. Result: `biology/developmental_action_sampling_probe_result.json`. This is an identifiability result in a supplied four-action toy, not evidence that infant play is intrinsically rewarding or that organisms implement a four-edge learner. The target setpoint, action effects, reversal, and support threshold are supplied; no growth or real pruning was measured.

**Boundary for the life principle.** Need/allostasis can specify which direction protects viability; efference copy makes a self-caused change attributable; developmental movement supplies interventions that passive observation cannot. Only repeated, action-conditioned prediction should increase local persistence, while unsupported edges decay under competition. The probe's failure mode is informative: with no varied interventions, the system can keep one edge alive while never identifying the post-reversal causal alternative. Test future digital rules by reporting both viability/action accuracy and edge identification/retention under delayed feedback, rather than action choice alone.

**Source opened with `lit.py fetch`.** Chen & Monroy (2026), “Moving, Seeing, Hearing, Smelling and Tasting: How Sensory-Motor Experiences Shape Early Cognitive Development,” *Behavioral Sciences* 16:255, PMCID [PMC12937931](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12937931/). The review's cited developmental studies are not evidence for a single endogenous reward signal.
