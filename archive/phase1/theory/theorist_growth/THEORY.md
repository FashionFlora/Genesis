# Growth by retained predictive contact

An edge is not allocated in advance. When a previously active unit meets a currently active unit, the pair exchanges one unit of contact resource. Its retained resource is also its predictive evidence. Between contacts, resource is spent on maintenance by the single saturating law `m <- m*m/(m+1)`. Resource below a small numerical floor is discarded; this is the finite representation of depletion, not a separate pruning signal. Thus contact births an edge and absence lets its predictive mass and existence decay together. Prediction selects the strongest extant incoming edge.

This does not yet claim that raw sensor channels themselves are grown; the judge fixes observation width. Report active units separately from allocated connection state, and test sparse initialization and phase-dependent edge mass.
